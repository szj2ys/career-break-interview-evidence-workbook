# My Career Break Interview Evidence Worksheet

Save or print a private copy. Replace the bracketed prompts with your own notes, or leave them blank where you do not know the answer. Do not publish personal or confidential material. A completed fictional version is in [worked-example.md](worked-example.md).

## 1. Choose a role and one requirement

Target role: [role]

Date of this worksheet: [date]

Source of the requirement: [job description, conversation, or explicitly labeled assumption]

One requirement to demonstrate: [quote or summarize it]

What does this requirement mean in practical work? [one task or decision]

What question might ask for it? [a likely question, not a claim about the actual interview]

## 2. Decide your privacy boundary

Period I want to explain, if relevant: [dates or broad period]

Context I am comfortable sharing: [one accurate phrase, or no personal detail]

Details I prefer to keep private: [private note; do not put this into the answer]

A sentence that redirects to the role while answering the question: [your wording]

Keep this separate from the skill example. You do not need to turn your personal circumstances into a workplace achievement.

## 3. Inventory the evidence before writing claims

For each entry, distinguish your actions from the team's actions. “Evidence source” can be a private note or an honest recollection; it is not a demand to upload documents.

### Evidence A — experience before the break

- When and in what setting: [date or period; paid work, volunteer work, study or other]
- Relevant task or problem: [what needed doing]
- My specific contribution: [what I decided or did]
- Other people's contribution: [what was not mine]
- Observed result: [what actually happened; use a number only if you can support it]
- Evidence source and access limits: [what supports the account; what must remain confidential]
- What this can demonstrate: [narrow supported claim]
- What it cannot demonstrate now: [recency, scale, tool or context limitation]

### Evidence B — recent preparation or practice

- When and in what setting: [date; self-directed exercise, course, volunteering or other]
- Task completed: [what you actually finished]
- My specific contribution: [your work]
- Feedback or check performed: [who checked what, or your own check]
- Evidence source: [private practice file, notes or other]
- What this can demonstrate: [narrow supported claim]
- What it cannot demonstrate: [for example, current paid-work experience or use under production pressure]

### Requirement still unsupported

- Claim I should not make yet: [unsupported claim]
- Small next step: [a specific exercise, question or preparation task]
- What would count as completion: [an observable output or answer]
- What would remain unproven afterward: [honest limit]

## 4. Check the claims you are tempted to make

| Proposed claim | Evidence A, B, or neither? | Accurate version | Detail to remove or qualify |
|---|---|---|---|
| [claim 1] | [source] | [supported wording] | [limit] |
| [claim 2] | [source] | [supported wording] | [limit] |

Check especially: “expert,” “up to date,” “led,” “improved,” “ready immediately,” and “used professionally.” These may be true, but each needs its own support.

## 5. Answer card A — explain the return

Question I am answering: [for example, “What brings you back to this kind of role?”]

Write a few sentences in your own voice:

1. Context I choose to share: [brief, accurate context]
2. Why this role interests me now: [motivation connected to actual work]
3. Relevant experience and recent preparation: [keep the dates/settings distinct]
4. Readiness or arrangements I can state accurately: [confirmed facts; label unresolved items]

My draft: [write the answer]

Check: Have I answered the question without promising availability, arrangements or proficiency that I have not established?

## 6. Answer card B — demonstrate the requirement

Question I am answering: [a behavioral question connected to section 1]

- Problem or constraint: [only the context needed to understand the decision]
- My action: [what you did and why]
- Result: [what happened, without adding a convenient metric]
- Boundary or reflection: [what the example does not prove, or what you learned]

My draft: [write the answer]

Check: Can a listener distinguish my contribution, the team's contribution and any recent practice that took place later?

## 7. Rehearse and listen for misunderstandings

Read each card aloud separately. A practice partner is optional. Timing is optional: clarity and truthful evidence are the purpose of this check.

- What might a listener incorrectly infer? [for example, that a practice exercise was paid client work]
- What did I repeat without adding useful context? [phrase or detail]
- What must remain because it supports the answer? [action, constraint, result or limitation]
- What is one fair follow-up question? [question]
- Can I answer it honestly now? [answer, or a gap to prepare]

Revised answer card A: [write the revision]

Revised answer card B: [write the revision]

## 8. Choose one next action

Next action: [specific task]

When I intend to do it: [your date or occasion]

Output I will keep privately: [practice artifact or note]

Claim I may be able to make afterward: [narrow claim]

Claim I still will not make: [remaining limitation]

Final check: both answer cards can stand without a link, an account, a score or an invented credential. Keep your completed copy private.
